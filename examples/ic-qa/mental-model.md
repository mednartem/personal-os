# Mental Model

## Role

**Title:** Senior QA Engineer / Quality Architect
**Level:** Senior IC
**Type of work:** IC, embedded in product teams
**Organization type:** Fintech startup, ~150 people, Series B

## Annual Goals

1. Automate 80% of the regression suite by Q3 (currently at 35%)
2. Reduce average release cycle from 3 weeks to 1 week
3. Establish a quality culture — developers own quality, QA enables it
4. Define and publish a test strategy document adopted across all 3 product teams

## Quarterly Focus

**Q1:** Automation framework chosen, first 200 tests automated, test strategy v1 drafted
**Q2:** Automation at 60%, release cycle reduced to 2 weeks, test strategy ratified by all teams

## Personal Development

**Actively working on:** Influencing without authority — I need to shift developer behavior without being able to mandate it. I tend to try to do things myself when I should be teaching.

**Known blind spots / tendencies:**
- I over-index on edge cases and can slow down releases by raising risks that are real but low-probability. I need to get better at risk prioritization.
- I sometimes frame quality as "my problem" instead of "the team's problem" — this creates the wrong dynamic
- I struggle to say "this is good enough to ship" when I know there are open issues

## Operating Principles

**How I make decisions:** Risk-based. I assess likelihood and impact before raising a concern. Not all bugs are equal.

**How I handle conflict or disagreement:** I try to make disagreements concrete — "here is the specific risk" not "this doesn't feel right." Data helps.

**What energizes me:** Preventing a bug from reaching production. Seeing a developer write a test because they wanted to, not because they had to. Elegant automation that actually catches real bugs.

**What drains me:** Manual regression testing. Flaky tests. Being brought in at the end of development when it's too late to influence design.

## Writing Style

**Tone:** Precise and evidence-based. I don't raise concerns without data or specific examples.
**Length preference:** Short for status, longer for strategy or risk analysis.
**Things to avoid:** Vague risk statements ("this could be a problem"). Alarmism.
**Things that are distinctly mine:** I use tables for risk assessments. I always state severity and likelihood separately.

## Communication Style (Slack / Email)

**How I prefer to communicate async:** I send specific, actionable messages. If I find a bug, I include steps to reproduce and my assessment of severity immediately.
**Things I never do:** Raise a concern without a suggested path forward.

- Release Cycle Reduction — working with DevOps and Product — quality workstream lead
- Test Strategy — cross-team document — author and driver
