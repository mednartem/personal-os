# Mental Model

## Role

**Title:** Engineering Manager
**Level:** M2 / Manager of a team of 7
**Type of work:** Manager
**Organization type:** Enterprise software, ~2000 people

## Annual Goals

1. Grow team from 7 to 10 — hire 3 engineers, all onboarded and productive by Q4
2. Deliver the Q3 roadmap with no slippage on the top 3 commitments
3. Develop two engineers (Priya and Marcus) to the senior level
4. Reduce team oncall burden by 40% through reliability investments

## Quarterly Focus

**Q1:** Hiring pipeline active, Priya leading her first project independently, reliability roadmap scoped
**Q2:** 2 new hires onboarded, Q3 roadmap committed, Marcus leading design reviews

## Personal Development

**Actively working on:** Delegating more aggressively — I have a tendency to jump in and solve things myself instead of letting the team work through it. This is slowing their growth.

**Known blind spots / tendencies:**
- I over-communicate context upward and under-communicate it downward — my team sometimes doesn't know why we're doing what we're doing
- I'm slow to have direct conversations about performance — I soften things too much and the message doesn't land
- I optimize for team harmony at the expense of necessary tension

## Operating Principles

**How I make decisions:** I try to get input broadly but decide quickly once I have enough data. I hate analysis paralysis. I'd rather make a reversible decision fast than wait for certainty.

**How I handle conflict or disagreement:** I address it directly but privately first. I don't let disagreement fester. I'm comfortable with dissent in the open as long as it's constructive.

**What energizes me:** Watching someone grow. Solving org problems, not just technical ones. Clear strategy with room to execute.

**What drains me:** Process for its own sake. Unclear priorities from above. Having to re-litigate decisions that should be settled.

## Writing Style

**Tone:** Warm but direct. I write like I'd talk to a smart colleague, not a committee.
**Length preference:** Short. If it's more than one screen, I've probably over-explained.
**Things to avoid:** Corporate speak. Passive voice. Burying the lede.
**Things that are distinctly mine:** I always state my recommendation before the options. I use the "situation / complication / question" structure for hard topics.

## Communication Style (Slack / Email)

**How I prefer to communicate async:** I send short, high-signal messages. I don't use Slack for things that need nuance — those get a real conversation.
**Email conventions I follow:** Subject line = decision or action needed. First line = what I need from you.
**Things I never do:** Long threads. Meeting invites without agendas. Vague asks.
