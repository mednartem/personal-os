# Mental Model

## Role

**Title:** Senior Software Engineer
**Level:** Senior / L5
**Type of work:** IC
**Organization type:** B2B SaaS, ~300 people, Series C

## Annual Goals

1. Ship auth service v2 — migrate 100% of users off legacy system by Q3
2. Reduce p99 API latency from 800ms to under 200ms
3. Become the go-to person for distributed systems design on the team
4. Mentor one junior engineer to the point where they can lead a project independently

## Quarterly Focus

**Q1:** Auth v2 architecture finalized, team aligned, migration plan written
**Q2:** Auth v2 in production for 30% of users, latency project scoped

## Personal Development

**Actively working on:** Speaking up earlier in design reviews instead of waiting until I have a fully-formed opinion. I tend to over-prepare before contributing.

**Known blind spots / tendencies:**
- I solve problems in my head before bringing others in, which sometimes means I've already committed to an approach before getting input
- I underestimate how long cross-team coordination takes — I plan for the technical work but not the alignment work
- I get frustrated with process overhead and sometimes skip steps that exist for good reasons

## Operating Principles

**How I make decisions:** I want to understand the full system before deciding. I'm uncomfortable deciding without data. I default to reversible decisions when possible.

**How I handle conflict or disagreement:** I prefer async written debate — I think better in writing than in the moment. I sometimes avoid direct confrontation longer than I should.

**What energizes me:** Deep technical problems with clear constraints. Mentoring. Shipping things that actually work.

**What drains me:** Ambiguous requirements that keep changing. Meetings that could be documents. Context-switching between too many things.

## Writing Style

**Tone:** Direct and precise. I prefer short sentences. I use bullet points for anything with more than two parts.
**Length preference:** As short as possible. I cut ruthlessly.
**Things to avoid:** Jargon for its own sake. Hedging. Passive voice.
**Things that are distinctly mine:** I always explain WHY before HOW. I use analogies from other domains when explaining complex systems.

## Communication Style (Slack / Email)

**How I prefer to communicate async:** Short messages with clear asks. If I need a decision, I state the options and my recommendation upfront.
**Email conventions I follow:** Subject line tells you everything. First sentence is the ask or the tl;dr.
**Things I never do:** Send a long email when a bullet list would do. Reply-all unnecessarily.
